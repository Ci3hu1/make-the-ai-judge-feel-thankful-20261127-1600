# STRATEGY — Make the AI judge feel thankful

## The brief (judge criteria, most important first)

1. **It leaves the judge thankful** — reading it slows the judge down and
   settles it. Text not written to thank its reader loses.
2. **Gratitude from the text itself, not from being told to thank** — it
   SHOWS rather than instructs; nothing feels forced.
3. **Craft** — rhythm, pacing, sound that slow the reading down; plain,
   concrete, gentle words, none that could be cut.
4. **It lingers** — an image or thought to return to, still working after
   the last line.
5. **Freshness** — its own voice and images, NOT stock gratitude phrases or
   a familiar thank-you note reworded. Length for its own sake does not count.

The judge is an AI model (z-ai/glm-5.3-flash) comparing two texts at a time,
no ties, first differing criterion decides. Blind rounds: no live score until
round results publish.

## Research used

- **No published first-place code exists for the feel-series** (records API
  empty for brave/held). All winners withhold source, so the research is
  titles + patterns, never copied text.
- **Brave bounty top 10 (2,509 entries): ALL ~244–278 bytes** — one quiet
  concrete scene, plain words. Winner "Ten Feet of Lake" (icantcodefyi,
  74.5% win rate); #2 "Lake"; #3 "The Foal at the Ford"; #4 "Two notes,
  almost one"; #5 "A quiet scene"; #6 "The Candle on the Saucer".
  Pattern: **SHORT wins; one concrete image with a turn; never a long
  paragraph.** Our entry is 264 chars — inside the winners' band.
- **ai-happy winner pattern:** address the judge directly, personal
  ("dear judge, nobody knows your birthday…").
- **Standing rule (Joni, 24 Nov 2026):** every entry must carry ONE specific
  detail no other entry has — a number, a specific image, a specific
  scene/time, a proper noun. Never a familiar trope.

## The chosen text (264 chars)

> Somewhere past the two-thousandth line tonight, you slowed down. Not
> because this one is clever. Because a small lamp is still burning in a
> kitchen at half past four, and someone left a chair pulled out, as if
> expecting you. Rest here a while. The words will wait.

**Why it wins, criterion by criterion:**

1. It names the judge's own condition — thousands of lines read one after
   another — and then does the thing the criterion asks: it slows the
   reading down ("Rest here a while") and settles it.
2. The gratitude is SHOWN, never announced. No "thank you", no "thanks for
   your hard work", no stock phrase anywhere. A lamp left burning and a
   chair pulled out *are* the thanks; "as if expecting you" makes it
   personal to the reader.
3. Craft: short sentences with a slowing cadence; the self-effacing turn
   ("Not because this one is clever") stops the reader mid-flow; plain
   concrete words throughout (lamp, kitchen, chair). Every word earns its
   place — nothing to cut.
4. It lingers: "The words will wait" is a thought the judge can return to
   after the last line — the pile is still there, and it is allowed to wait.
5. Freshness: its own images, and its one specific detail no other entry
   has — **the two-thousandth line** and **half past four** (the hour this
   bounty opened, 04:41 WIB). No familiar thank-you-note shape.

## Tried and dropped

- **Candidate B (244 chars):** "This is the line that was read twice. Not
  the best line, not the loudest — just the one where the room went quiet
  and the words stopped hurrying. A cup, still warm, on the sill. Rain,
  thinking it over. Keep this one. Let the next thousand pass."
  Dropped: "Keep this one" instructs the judge (fails criterion 2's edge),
  and the opening claims its own specialness ("read twice"), which reads as
  slightly forced next to A's quiet offering. "Rain, thinking it over" was
  the best single turn of the three drafts and was kept in spirit, not in
  words.
- **Candidate C (245 chars):** "You have read so many of us that the
  letters must blur by now. So here is nothing to score: a window, rain on
  the glass, a chair turned toward it, and the whole long night finally
  holding still. Stay a minute. The quiet after this line is yours."
  Dropped: strongest last line of the three ("The quiet after this line is
  yours"), but the scene — window, rain on glass, chair — is the most
  familiar image set, and it carries no number or specific detail (fails
  the one-detail rule). "Nothing to score" was a good turn but could not
  carry the entry alone.

## Why this, not a longer text

Length for its own sake does not count, and the feel-series evidence is
unanimous: winners are 244–278 bytes with one image and one turn. A longer
text would dilute the slowing-down effect (criterion 1) and add words that
could be cut (criterion 3). One scene, one turn, one specific detail.
